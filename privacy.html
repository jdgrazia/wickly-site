Wickly Privacy Policy

Effective October 10, 2026

Wickly is made by jdgrazia ("we"). This policy explains what the Wickly app collects and why. Questions: altjdgrazia@gmail.com.

The short version

  • Wickly has no accounts to make. We never ask for your real name, email or phone number. To keep our server safe, the app signs in to it by itself with a random ID.
  • Your watchlist, calls, verdicts and settings stay on your phone, apart from what your profile shows (below).
  • Each call you make adds one to an anonymous tally that can't be traced back to you.
  • If you join the leaderboard, the name you pick and your accuracy are shown to everyone, and you get a profile anyone on Wickly can open: your name, accuracy, how many follow you, your last 12 verdicts, and the photo and short bio you add if you add them. Players can find you by searching for your name.
  • If you follow friends or post cards, our server stores who you follow, your cards (with any picture or verdict you add) and your answers. Your followers see your cards (or just the ones you send a card to), and you see who answered yours.
  • A card can link to a YouTube video, which plays on the card in YouTube's own player. YouTube gets your IP address when it shows the video's thumbnail or you play it.
  • If you let Wickly tell you about new followers and friends' cards, our server stores your phone's push token so Google's Firebase can deliver those notifications.
  • If something goes wrong, the app sends us a report with technical details about your phone and the error.
  • On the Free plan, Google shows ads and, on Android, may use your device's advertising ID to do so.

What we collect

Anonymous call tallies

When you call a chart bullish or bearish, the app adds one to a count on our server for that asset, timeframe and hour. The count records whether the call was bullish or bearish and whether it was made before you knew what the asset was. Nothing else is stored with it: no name, account, device ID, advertising ID or location, so the tallies can't be linked to you. (The request carries your install's sign-in ID, described below, so the server can limit how many calls one install counts in an hour, but the ID isn't stored with the tallies.) To count each person once per chart, your phone remembers your own latest call and moves it if you call the same chart again.

We use the tallies to show how other people called a chart (part of Wickly Premium) and to improve Wickly. We may also share or license them in aggregate, for example as market sentiment data.

Leaderboard (only if you join)

The leaderboard is optional. When you join, you pick a name to show on it (it doesn't need to be your real name), and the app sends us that name, a random player ID it makes for you, and how many of your calls from the last 30 days have been judged and how many were right. It updates those numbers as your calls are judged. Everyone using Wickly can see your name and accuracy on the leaderboard; the player ID stays private. You can change your name or leave at any time in the app, and leaving deletes your entry. Players who don't join send none of this.

Your profile (only if you join the leaderboard)

With a leaderboard name, you have a profile. Anyone using Wickly can open it, by tapping your name or finding you in the search on the Friends screen, which matches names as they're typed. It shows your name, when you joined, how many players follow you and how many you follow, your accuracy over the last 30 days, and your last 12 verdicts: which stock, coin or bet each was, its name, your call on each chart and when you made it. For that, the app sends each verdict to our server once you've made it, and the server keeps your latest 12 and deletes older ones. Your other verdicts, and the calls you passed on, stay on your phone.

You can add a photo and a bio of up to 150 characters; both are optional, and you can change or remove them at any time. The app only gets the picture you pick, and before sending it, it crops it to a small square and removes the details photos carry, such as where and when they were taken and with what phone. Photos on profiles you see are kept in Wickly's cache on your phone so they don't download again. Bios go through the same word filter as cards.

You can report a player's photo or bio. You stop seeing them at once. A report sends us their name, their bio and the reason you pick, and we keep it only as long as we need to look into it. A photo and bio several players report are hidden from everyone until we've looked at them.

Friends and cards (only if you use them)

With a leaderboard name, you can follow other players by their names, and they can follow you by yours. An invite link you share from Friends carries your name, and opening it in Wickly follows you; the invite page on our website only uses the name to show it and to open Wickly. Our server stores who follows whom and who you've blocked. Players who follow each other are friends, and the Friends board shows you and your friends' names and accuracy.

You can send cards to your followers: a note, in the card design you pick, and a picture, GIF or screenshot if you add one, or a YouTube video if you paste its link (see YouTube videos on cards). The app only gets the picture you pick, and before sending it, it makes it smaller and removes the details photos carry, such as where and when they were taken and with what phone. A GIF keeps its frames: the app removes any comments it carries (such as what made it), and makes it smaller if it's over 5 MB. Pictures on the cards you see are kept in Wickly's cache on your phone for up to 8 days, so they don't download again; your phone can clear the cache at any time. A card you send to all your followers shows in each of their decks. Anyone who knows your name can follow you unless you block them; blocking also stops them seeing your cards. When your followers answer a card with a ❤️ or a 🚫, you see who sent which. When you answer someone's card, they see your answer; how others answered is only for them to see. You can also send a card to some of the people who follow you, or just one, like a message, and only they see it; when it goes to more than one, each of them sees how many others it went to, but not who. Anyone who can see one of your cards can reply to it, with a card just for you, even if you don't follow them, while your card is up; the reply shows the words of the card it answers, to the two of you. Blocking someone stops their replies. Sending a verdict to friends puts the verdict's card on your card: which stock, coin or bet it was, its name and your call on each chart. Cards and their answers are deleted 7 days after they're posted, or sooner if you delete them.

You can report a card that breaks the rules, including one sent just to you. A report sends us the card's question and verdict, its author's name and the reason you pick, and we keep it only as long as we need to look into it. A card several players report is hidden until we've looked at it, and we remove cards and players that break the rules.

Leaving the leaderboard deletes your profile (its photo, bio and verdicts), follows, blocks, cards and answers along with your entry.

Notifications about followers and cards (only if you allow them)

With a leaderboard name, Wickly can tell you when someone follows you, and when a friend sends you a card or posts or shares one, if your phone lets it show notifications. For that, our server stores your phone's push token (an ID Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging gives your phone for Wickly) with your random player ID and which of the two you want, and sends each notification through Firebase, which delivers it. A notification says who it's from ("Ann followed you on Wickly", "Ann sent you a card") and, for a card, its question. Turning off New followers and Cards from friends in Settings, or leaving the leaderboard, deletes the token from our server.

Problem and crash reports

When you send a report from Settings, it includes what you write, the email address you add (if any), and details that help us fix the problem: your phone's make and model, its Android or iOS version, Wickly's version, screen and font size, language, your plan, whether notifications are on, and the app's recent log (its own technical messages, which can mention charts you looked at). If Wickly crashes or runs into an error, it sends a report with the error and the same details automatically, without a message or email. The screen where you send a report shows exactly what goes with it. We use reports only to find and fix problems, and they're deleted after 90 days.

Subscriptions

Wickly Plus and Wickly Premium are sold through Google Play on Android and the App Store on iPhone. Google or Apple handles payment, so we never see your payment details. The app checks with the store whether you have an active subscription.

Ads

On the Free plan, ads are served by Google AdMob. To show and measure ads, Google may collect information such as your device's advertising ID, your IP address and how you interact with ads. Learn how Google uses this information at https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites. On Android, you can reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings. On iPhone, Wickly doesn't ask for permission to track you, so its ads don't use your advertising identifier. Wickly Plus and Wickly Premium have no ads.

Market data

Charts come from Yahoo Finance and Polymarket. The app asks them for prices directly, so they receive standard request information such as your IP address. We don't send them anything else about you.

YouTube videos on cards

When a card links to a YouTube video, the video plays on the card, through YouTube API Services. The card shows the video's thumbnail, which comes from YouTube, and tapping it loads YouTube's own player, in its privacy-enhanced mode (from youtube-nocookie.com). YouTube receives standard request information such as your IP address, and what its player collects while you watch; we don't send it anything else about you. Watching a video in Wickly means YouTube's Terms of Service apply (https://www.youtube.com/t/terms), and Google's Privacy Policy covers what YouTube collects (https://policies.google.com/privacy). Links in the player, like the video's title, open in YouTube's app or your browser.

Sign-in ID

To protect our server from abuse, the app signs in to it by itself, with no screen and nothing for you to enter. Our hosting provider's sign-in service (Supabase Auth) gives your install a random sign-in ID, and keeps it with when it was made and last used and, for security, the IP address and app details it signs in from. The app sends the sign-in with every request to our server. The sign-in ID isn't stored with your leaderboard entry, cards or the call tallies. The server only uses it to count how many calls, searches, profile verdicts, new leaderboard players, problem reports and pictures one install makes or downloads in an hour or a day, so no one can flood Wickly, and it deletes those counts after two days. A sign-in ID that hasn't been used for 90 days is deleted, with what Supabase keeps with it.

Server logs

Like any website, our hosting provider (Supabase) keeps short-lived request logs, which include IP addresses, for security and troubleshooting. They are not added to the tallies.

What stays on your phone

Your watchlist, calls, verdicts (apart from the last 12 on your profile, if you have one), settings, your own latest call on each chart and the record of which calls were right are stored only on your device, and uninstalling Wickly deletes them. The app judges your calls on your phone, using the same market data as the charts. Verdict notifications are created on your phone; we don't send them. Only notifications about followers and friends' cards come from our server.

When you share a verdict, the app draws the image on your phone and hands it to the app you pick, or saves it to your photos if you tap the save button. We don't receive it.

While your verdict pack, your past verdicts or a friend's verdict card is on your screen, the cards shine as you tilt your phone, using the phone's motion sensor. Those readings are used on the spot and are never stored or sent.

Deleting your data

  • Your leaderboard entry, profile, follows, blocks, cards, answers and push token: in Wickly, tap the trophy, then ⋮, then Leave the leaderboard. That deletes them from our server straight away. All that's kept is the random player ID, which no longer leads to anything, so the app can't bring your old entry back.
  • Everything on your phone: uninstall Wickly, or clear its storage in your phone's settings.
  • Your sign-in ID: it's deleted once it goes unused for 90 days, so after you uninstall Wickly it goes too.
  • Just your photo or bio: remove them from your profile in Wickly (Friends, then Your profile, then Edit your photo and bio). They're deleted from our server straight away.
  • Problem and crash reports: email altjdgrazia@gmail.com with roughly when you sent the report, or the email address you added to it, and we'll delete it.
  • If you can't use the app: email altjdgrazia@gmail.com with your leaderboard name, and we'll delete your entry and everything that goes with it.

The anonymous call tallies can't be linked to you, so there's nothing of yours in them to delete.

Links to other services

Trade buttons open Robinhood or Coinbase. Some links include affiliate tracking, which tells that service the visit came from Wickly. Their own privacy policies cover what happens there.

Children

Wickly is for adults. It isn't meant for anyone under 18.
